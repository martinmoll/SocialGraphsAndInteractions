We built this project with an AI assistant at almost every step: it wrote most of the code, proposed several of
the checks and drafted much of the text. That made the boxes more relevant than we expected. Each box shows a
fluent paragraph with a hidden gap, and an AI-drafted report is fluent by default.

The week 8 box was the one that applied most directly. Its example reports a modularity with no null and never
compares its methods. Our first prototype produced a clean headline within a day, and without the box's warning in
mind it would have read the same way: one network more divided than another, with no baseline. The final paper
compares each map with three nulls, compares maps only at equal size, and says how much of the floor map's
division comes from debate procedure.

The week 7 box [Diego: check the box's exact wording on the week 7 page before keeping this], on counting names that are not resolved people, described our name matcher. Thirty printed
matches looked right. Counting a hundred showed seven wrong, mostly in the direction of our finding, and that led to
the matcher fixes and a measured precision and recall. The box on trusting a classifier's accuracy matched what we
found with our own: it scored 73% against 72% for always guessing the most common label, so we did not use it.
