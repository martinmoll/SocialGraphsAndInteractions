                        TRIBUTE TO RAMON VICIOSO

                                  _____
                                 

                            HON. DEREK TRAN

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, October 21, 2025

  Mr. TRAN. Mr. Speaker, it is with solemn remembrance that I rise 
today to pay tribute to a beloved husband, devoted father, thoughtful 
grandfather, and courageous Purple Heart Veteran, Ramon Vicioso. Ramon, 
known as Ray to those close to him, passed away peacefully on September 
28, 2025, at the age of 93.
  Ray was born in New York City, grew up in the Bronx borough, and 
enlisted in the United States Marine Corp on October 23, 1947 at the 
age of 17 with his father's permission. He was first stationed at Camp 
Pendleton in Southern California before being sent to the frontlines of 
the Korean War. In July 1951, his tank platoon in Korea was traveling 
down a road when foot soldiers came up behind them. The tanks were 
ordered to pull off the road, unknowingly into a mine field, to let the 
soldiers pass. Unfortunately, Ray's tank was one of the first to be 
blown up, which resulted in his left leg being amputated and his right 
leg severely damaged. Upon returning to California, Ray continued his 
recovery, was awarded the Korean Service Medal, and received a Purple 
Heart for his sacrifice in the war.
  As he transitioned back into civilian life in the 1960's, he began 
his long career in aerospace planning for Bendix and later Martin 
Marietta, supporting their critical efforts in the race to outer space 
as well as working on the latter's Titan IV launch team. Ray retired in 
Nevada where he was an active member of the local veteran community. He 
was known for his unwavering pride in the Marines and his own service, 
never complaining about what happened to him in Korea.
  Ray was also a beloved husband, father, and grandfather. While his 
wife of over 40 years, Patricia Vicioso, passed away in 2024, he is 
survived by his five children, Victoria Hoffman, Ted Vicioso, David 
Vicioso, Paul Iverson, and one of my constituents, Beth Brown, as well 
as several grandchildren and great-grandchildren. I have requested that 
the United States flag be flown over our Nation's Capital in 
recognition of Ray's life and sacrifice to this country. Ray will be 
dearly missed by his friends and family, and his fiery legacy will 
continue on in the hearts of everyone who knew him.
