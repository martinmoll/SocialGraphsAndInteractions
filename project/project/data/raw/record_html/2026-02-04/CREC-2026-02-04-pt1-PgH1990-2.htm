                   ICE'S TERRORIZATION ACROSS AMERICA

  (Mrs. McCLAIN DELANEY of Maryland was recognized to address the House 
for 5 minutes.)
  Mrs. McCLAIN DELANEY. Mr. Speaker, I rise today as tensions across 
this country and in this very Chamber have reached a boiling point.
  Yesterday, many of us, freshmen Members like myself and those Members 
with decades in this Chamber, faced one of the hardest votes any of us 
will ever take. We were forced to decide on a vote on a bill that 
bundled funding for ICE and DHS with the remainder of our government 
appropriations.
  This is because we all know, Republicans and Democrats alike, that 
this administration is using ICE as a taxpayer-funded paramilitary 
force that is holding itself above the law with no transparency, 
accountability, or guardrails.
  We see the carnage every day and the consequences everywhere. Federal 
immigration agents are terrorizing our communities, including last 
month in Minnesota, where two 37-year-olds, both Renee Good and Alex 
Pretti, were killed in separate shootings during the 9-week siege and 
occupation of Minnesota.

                              {time}  1145

  Now, 2 weeks ago, I traveled with 27 other House Members to Minnesota 
to a field hearing to hear firsthand the accounts from the victims and 
the advocates about ICE's abuses. I was jarred, and I am still troubled 
by their testimonies, including vans set up to stop all passersby for 
their papers; the dragging of a pregnant woman through the streets; the 
seizure of an elderly grandmother with asylum, pulled from her car and 
leaving two grandchildren in a running car, in ice cold temperatures, 
in the back seat; the shackling and detention of observers who merely 
honked their horn or blew whistles or took pictures; and detention 
holding pens of up to 50 people without adequate food, water, or 
bathroom breaks.
  Now, I stand with Minnesota because what is happening in Minnesota 
has cruel consequences everywhere, including in my home State of 
Maryland. In my district, Maryland's Sixth, the administration covertly 
just acquired a $2 million warehouse in Williamsport to build a 1,500-
bed processing center with no public or congressional input.
  ICE Acting Director Todd Lyons likens these warehouses in 
Williamsport and nationwide to Amazon Prime facilities but for living 
human beings.
  Let me be clear: People are not packages. Let me say it again. People 
are

[[Page H1991]]

not packages. I stand with Governor Moore and Maryland's Democratic 
delegation to fight these plans, and I laud community leaders and those 
across the U.S. who are mobilizing to block these warehouses and other 
detention centers which are really private prisons.
  A viral video surfaced showing ungodly conditions within ICE's 
Baltimore field office, a facility where my Sixth District constituents 
and other Marylanders are being detained.
  For over 2 months, I sought to gain access to this facility to 
conduct a congressional oversight tour to see my constituents. Last 
Friday, after multiple unexplained cancellations by ICE, I finally 
gained access. What I found was truly disturbing. My team and I are 
still processing the horrendous conditions we saw in a holding site for 
temporary detention of 12 hours but which stretched for multiple days.
  Detainees had precious little due process. They were forced to sleep 
on thin mats with foil blankets. They had a communal sink and toilet. I 
saw individuals behind thick walls signaling, rubbing their bellies, 
because they were hungry.
  After pressing ICE, I also confirmed that the viral video was true. I 
asked to also speak with these detainees, but they would not allow me.
  But I refuse to stay silent. In this Chamber, we have to unite around 
a shared principle. We all want our laws and immigration laws to be 
enforced but transparently, fairly, humanely, and in accordance with 
our values.
  I want to make clear where I stand. I did not and will not vote on 
any bill that funds ICE until the American people and Congress have 
clear answers and meaningful change.
  What we need is transparency and accountability, starting with the 
impeachment of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem. Time and again, 
she has proved her incompetency.
  I am also joining with my colleagues to cosponsor legislation, 
including banning ICE agents from wearing masks, requiring body cams 
for law enforcement agencies, and not deporting American citizens, as 
well as respecting constitutional laws and due process. These are 
commonsense measures we all should and must support.
  In closing, this is not about values but who we are as a country. On 
my exit from the ICE Baltimore facility, a career DHS employee said: 
This is not who I am but what I do. I retorted: What we do is who we 
are.
  Our country deserves transparency, humanity, and a respect for our 
constitutional laws and individual rights. I hope my Republican 
colleagues have the courage to stand up. Enough is enough.
