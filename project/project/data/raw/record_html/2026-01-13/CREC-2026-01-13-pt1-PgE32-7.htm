HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF COUNCILWOMAN DOROTHY ``MS. DOT'' GLENN 
                                NICHOLS

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. BENNIE G. THOMPSON

                             of mississippi

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, January 13, 2026

  Mr. THOMPSON of Mississippi. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and 
celebrate the extraordinary life, service, and legacy of Councilwoman 
Dorothy ``Ms. Dot'' Glenn Nichols, a devoted public servant, community 
leader, educator, and compassionate advocate for the people of 
Greenwood, Leflore County, and the State of Mississippi.
  Councilwoman Glenn Nichols was born on October 16, 1957, in Leflore 
County, Mississippi. From an early age she demonstrated a steadfast 
commitment to faith, service, and education. She was a lifelong member 
of New Foundation Missionary Baptist Church in Greenwood, Mississippi, 
where she faithfully served in numerous leadership roles and lived out 
her Christian values through service to others.
  A proud graduate of Greenwood High School, Class of 1976, 
Councilwoman Glenn Nichols pursued higher education with purpose and 
determination. She earned an Associate of Arts Degree in Education from 
Mary Holmes Junior College and later a Bachelor of Science Degree in 
Early Childhood Development, with a minor in Business Administration, 
from Tougaloo College. Her educational foundation became the 
cornerstone of a distinguished career devoted to improving the lives of 
children and families.
  For decades, Ms. Glenn Nichols served in the Head Start and Early 
Start programs at the local, state, and national levels. Beginning

[[Page E33]]

as a classroom teacher, she rose through the ranks to serve as a case 
worker, child and family advocate, program lead, and ultimately a Head 
Start Technical Assistance Program Improvement Coach. Her expertise 
supported educators, administrators, and agencies throughout 
Mississippi and the broader region. Her work gained national 
recognition, leading to her appointment as a National Advisor and 
Professional Development Specialist with the Council for Professional 
Recognition, where she helped shape the future of early childhood 
education across Mississippi, Arkansas, and Alabama.
  Beyond her professional achievements, Councilwoman Glenn Nichols was 
a devoted entrepreneur and community builder. As the owner and operator 
of Dot's Corner Market on historic Broad Street in Greenwood, she 
transformed a neighborhood store into a center of care, connection, and 
compassion. Known affectionately as ``The Mother of Broad Street,'' she 
provided not only goods, but guidance, encouragement, and refuge for 
countless individuals. Her store became a symbol of trust, unity, and 
resilience within the community.
  Councilwoman Glenn Nichols also answered the call to public service. 
She represented the citizens of Greenwood with integrity and dedication 
as a member of the Greenwood City Council. In 2021, she assumed the 
role of Councilwoman for District 6 and was re-elected in 2025, serving 
faithfully until her passing. Her leadership was rooted in advocacy, 
fairness, and an unwavering commitment to uplifting her constituents. 
She was a lifelong member of Sigma Gamma Rho Sorority, Incorporated, 
the NAACP Leflore County Chapter, and the Greenwood Voters League--
organizations that reflect her dedication to civic engagement, social 
justice, and community empowerment.
  On Friday, January 2, 2026, Councilwoman Dorothy ``Ms. Dot'' Glenn 
Nichols departed this life, leaving behind a legacy defined by faith, 
service, leadership, and love. Her impact is evident in the lives of 
the children she educated, the families she supported, the community 
she strengthened, and the city she served.
  Mr. Speaker, Councilwoman Glenn Nichols exemplified what it means to 
live a life of purpose. Her compassion, perseverance, and commitment to 
others stand as an enduring example of public service at its finest. I 
ask my colleagues to join me in honoring her life and legacy.
