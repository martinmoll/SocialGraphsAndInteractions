   CELEBRATING THE 87TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE ALBANY SOUTHWEST GEORGIAN

                                  _____
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, December 5, 2025

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor The Albany Southwest 
Georgian on its 87th year of continuous publication and dedicated 
service to the people of Southwest Georgia. An event to celebrate this 
singular milestone will be held on December 5, 2025, at Albany 
Technical College in Albany, Georgia. The Keynote Speaker for this 
event will be the publisher of the Westside Gazette, Mr. Bobby Henry. 
The theme for this event is ``Old enough to know, but young enough to 
grow.''
  Founded on August 8, 1938, by Virgil Hodges and A.C. Searles, The 
Albany Southwest Georgian has long served as a vital voice for the 
African American community in Albany and the surrounding region. For 
nearly nine decades the paper has reported with integrity and 
independence, chronicling local life, preserving community memory, and 
advocating for civic engagement and public service.
  Throughout its storied history, The Albany Southwest Georgian has 
been led by committed stewards who sustained its mission across 
generations. Under the leadership of the late Commissioner Arthur 
Searles and later Giselle Searles, the newspaper navigated social, 
cultural, and political changes while remaining rooted in accuracy and 
community uplift. In May 2019, stewardship of this proud institution 
passed to former State Representative Winfred Dukes and his mother, 
Mrs. Willie Beatrice Dukes, who continue to preserve its legacy and 
ensure its service to future generations.
  The Albany Southwest Georgian played a vital role during the Albany 
Civil Rights Movement, documenting mass meetings, demonstrations, and 
the tireless grassroots organizing that helped shape the national 
struggle for justice and equality. In times of crisis, such as the 
catastrophic floods of 1994 and 1998, and the recent COVID-19 pandemic, 
the newspaper served as a trusted source of information and a 
stabilizing presence for local families and civic leaders. Our former 
colleague, Civil Rights Icon John Lewis once said that ``We need the 
press now more than ever before to be a headlight and not a 
taillight.'' The Albany Southwest Georgian has and will continue to be 
a headlight that will shine a light on truth, justice and equality for 
all of God's children.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me and my wife, Vivian in 
congratulating The Albany Southwest Georgian on 87 years of 
distinguished service to Southwest Georgia. We commend its record of 
faithful journalism, community advocacy, and historical preservation.
  May The Albany Southwest Georgian continue to inform, inspire, and 
strengthen the communities it serves for many years to come.
