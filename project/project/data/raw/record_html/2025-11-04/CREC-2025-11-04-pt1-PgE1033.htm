                       HONORING MS. NAYLA McCLURE

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. BENNIE G. THOMPSON

                             of mississippi

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 4, 2025

  Mr. THOMPSON of Mississippi. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a 
tenacious and self-motivated leader, Ms. Nayla McClure. She has shown 
what can be achieved through hard work, dedication, and a steadfast 
commitment to change.
  For Ms. McClure, the path to advocacy did not begin with a grand plan 
to change the world. Instead, it began with a willingness to listen, 
adapt, and act. A native of Utica, Mississippi, she was recently 
recognized for her leadership in developing The People's Water Project, 
an initiative designed to address water accessibility in Utica and 
neighboring communities while creating a blueprint that other towns can 
follow.
  Ms. McClure received a $20,000 grant from the Southern Poverty Law 
Center's Advocacy Institute, an annual nine-week program that trains 
fellows across Mississippi in practical advocacy skills. When she first 
joined the Institute, her project was not centered on water. The shift 
came after reflecting on the dire needs of her community and the 
realities of Mississippi's ongoing water crisis.
  The project provides immediate relief by ensuring residents have 
access to clean, safe water, while also focusing on long-term 
solutions. Ms. McClure is developing an impact report and a Water 
Justice Toolkit to bridge the gap between policy and community.
  For her, the work is deeply personal. Communities like Utica often 
lack the funding and infrastructure necessary to sustain basic 
services, highlighting the need for equitable resource allocation. From 
intern to national policy analyst, Ms. McClure's journey has been 
shaped by both academic study and hands-on experience. She began as an 
intern with the Mississippi Public Service Commission and now serves as 
a full-time policy analyst with national reach.
  As Mississippi continues to grapple with aging infrastructure and 
ongoing water challenges, leaders like Nayla McClure remind us that 
solutions do not always come from the top down. Sometimes they begin 
with listening, adapting, and acting, one community at a time.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing Ms. Nayla 
McClure for her dedication and service to her community, and for 
reminding us all that true leadership begins with courage, compassion, 
and the will to act on behalf of others.
