HONORING THE SERVICE, SACRIFICE, AND LEGACY OF FIRST LIEUTENANT GERALD 
                              J. MELOFCHIK

                                 ______
                                 

                       HON. BRIAN K. FITZPATRICK

                            of pennsylvania

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 4, 2025

  Mr. FITZPATRICK. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life, legacy, 
and ultimate sacrifice of United States Army Air Forces First 
Lieutenant Gerald J. Melofchik. He was a brave American who gave his 
life in defense of freedom during World War II and whose remains were 
finally accounted for on June 24, 2025, by the Defense Prisoner of War/
Missing in Action (POW/MIA) Accounting Agency (DPAA).
  Born and raised in New York City, First Lieutenant Gerald Melofchik 
embodied the extraordinary patriotism and sense of duty that has long 
defined America's Greatest Generation. Assigned to the 615th 
Bombardment Squadron, 401st Bombardment Group (Heavy), Eighth United 
States Army Air Forces, First Lieutenant Melofchik served as a B-17G 
``Flying Fortress'' pilot in the European Theater of Operations. On 
August 1, 1944, during a perilous bombing mission over Nazi-occupied 
France, his aircraft that was nicknamed ``Stormy Weather'', was struck 
by anti-aircraft fire, collided with another aircraft, and crashed near 
Neron, France. Of the nine courageous airmen aboard, two were captured 
and four were identified from remains found near the crash site. Three 
airmen, including First Lieutenant Melofchik remained unaccounted for.
  For eight decades, his family lived with uncertainty, holding on to 
faith that he would one day be returned to American soil. His mother 
never stopped believing that her son would come home.
  In 2013, DPAA investigators surveyed the crash site in Neron, mapping 
a vast debris field consistent with an American World War II air loss. 
From 2021 to 2023, DPAA partnered with Middle Tennessee State 
University to excavate the site. Through meticulous scientific analysis 
that included anthropological review and mitochondrial, Y-chromosome, 
and autosomal DNA analysis performed with the Armed Forces Medical 
Examiner System, First Lieutenant Melofchik was finally identified.

[[Page E1037]]

After 81 years, America now keeps her promise of leaving no one behind.
  First Lieutenant Melofchik's name is inscribed on the Wall of the 
Missing at the Brittany American Cemetery in Montjoie Saint Martin, 
France. Soon, a rosette will be placed beside his name, signifying that 
he has been accounted for and returned to his country.
  But behind the uniform was a beloved son, brother, uncle, and young 
man with dreams. Gerald was the son of Stephen and Pauline, and brother 
to Stephen, Marie, and Henry. Though his 18 nieces and nephews never 
had the privilege of knowing him in life, they grew up honoring his 
memory through stories of a young man with a competitive spirit on the 
tennis court, a fondness for Hershey's chocolate bars, and a heart that 
deeply loved his family and his country.
  His life was short, but his impact endures. His courage helped 
liberate a continent that was occupied for several years under the 
Nazis. His sacrifice helped preserve the freedoms we enjoy today. And 
now, generations later, he is welcomed home with full honors, dignity, 
and profound gratitude.
  Mr. Speaker, let it be known that the United States of America does 
not forget her heroes. Today, we honor First Lieutenant Gerald J. 
Melofchik, who was a patriot flying into danger so that others might 
live in peace. May his memory live forever in the hearts of the Nation 
he served.
  On behalf of a grateful nation, we welcome home First Lieutenant 
Gerald J. Melofchik. His mission is complete. His nation will always 
remember. His courage will never be forgotten. I thank him for his 
service and sacrifice.
