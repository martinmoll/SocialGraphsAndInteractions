  HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF THE HONORABLE FOFO IOSEFO FITI SUNIA

                                 ______
                                 

                   HON. AUMUA AMATA COLEMAN RADEWAGEN

                           of american samoa

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 4, 2025

  Mrs. RADEWAGWEN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and 
service of High Talking Chief Fofo Iosefo Fiti Sunia, the first 
Delegate from American Samoa to the United States Congress, who passed 
away on October 14, 2025, at the age of 88. Born on March 13, 1937, in 
Fagasa, Pago Pago, Fofo was a lifelong servant of the people of 
American Samoa. He graduated from Samoana High School and earned a 
degree in economics from the University of Hawaii before returning home 
to begin a career marked by leadership and public service. He served as 
translator and interpreter for the territorial governor, Director of 
Tourism, Election Commissioner, Senator for Manu`a District No. 1, and 
Director of the Legislative Reference Bureau. Elected to Congress in 
1980, Fofo served four consecutive terms from 1981 to 1988, where he 
championed legislation to strengthen education, infrastructure, and 
economic development in the islands. He was instrumental in securing 
recognition for children of American Samoans born abroad and was known 
for his steadfast commitment to protecting Samoan culture, traditions, 
and land rights. Beyond his public service, Fofo was a renowned author, 
historian, and cultural advocate. His writings and televised programs 
helped preserve and promote the Samoan language, culminating in the 
establishment of the Samoan Language Commission. Among his many works 
are Puputoa, Timata le Gagana, and Auafa o Upu: Treasure Trove of Rare 
Samoan Words.
  Congressman Sunia was a dear friend and counselor to me. I cherish 
his memory and kindness. He was helpful as I followed in his footsteps 
as a Representative in Washington, where he was our first under the 
current rules, although we sent representatives beforehand too that did 
not have the privileges of Members of Congress. I saw him always as a 
mentor, advisor, and counselor. I benefited from his prior role as a 
representative for our territory, and I remember that he too took pride 
in decorating his D.C. office to reflect our South Pacific home and 
culture. He worked for important rights such as nominating our youth to 
the U.S. Service Academies, including our first ever successful nominee 
and graduate, my Chief of Staff Leafaina Tavai, so his work was 
foundational for many future successes, including our Medicaid program. 
He had the wisdom of experience and a gentle but effective way of 
expressing his opinion and views. He was an accomplished author, and 
his books are valuable additions to our library and schools. He will be 
deeply missed. May God bless his family and loved ones in this time. 
American Samoa honors him as a public servant and statesman. God has 
welcomed him home as a man of faith.
