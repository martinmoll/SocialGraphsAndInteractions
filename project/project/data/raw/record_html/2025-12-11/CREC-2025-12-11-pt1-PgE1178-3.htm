                  HONORING THE LIFE OF THOMAS O. HICKS

                                  _____
                                 

                           HON. RYAN K. ZINKE

                               of montana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, December 11, 2025

  Mr. ZINKE. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor the life and legacy of Thomas 
O. Hicks.
  Thomas O. Hicks, of Dallas, Texas, was a devoted husband, father, and 
grandfather, a patriotic American, and a respected leader in business, 
philanthropy, and civic life.
  Mr. Hicks served his country in the United States Army Reserve and 
later as a presidentially appointed Commissioner of the American Battle 
Monuments Commission, helping to honor and preserve the memory of 
United States service members at military cemeteries and memorials 
around the world.
  Mr. Hicks was widely regarded as a pioneer in American private 
equity, co-founding Hicks & Haas in 1984 and later Hicks, Muse, Tate & 
Furst in 1989, and helping to advance the ``buy-and-build'' strategy 
that transformed the leveraged buyout model and created tens of 
thousands of American jobs in consumer products, broadcasting, and food 
and beverage industries.
  Mr. Hicks made extraordinary contributions to the city of Dallas and 
to the State of Texas, including helping to develop the American 
Airlines Center, supporting the construction of the Margaret Hunt Hill 
Bridge, and championing education initiatives such as the land donation 
that became Thomas O. Hicks Elementary in the Lewisville Independent 
School District.
  Mr. Hicks brought distinction to American sports through his 
ownership and leadership of the Dallas Stars from 1995 to 2011, 
including the 1999 Stanley Cup Championship, and the Texas Rangers from 
1998 to 2010, during which the team won multiple American League West 
Division titles and reached the World Series.
  Mr. Hicks remained deeply committed to higher education and to the 
University of Texas, where he served on the Board of Regents from 1994 
to 1999 and helped establish the University of Texas Investment 
Management Company (UTIMCO), which has grown into the largest public 
university endowment in the Nation.
  Above all his accomplishments in business, sports, and civic life, 
Mr. Hicks was proudest of his role as a family man, dedicating his life 
to his wife, children, and grandchildren, and instilling in them a deep 
love of family, country, and community.
  He is survived by his beloved wife of 35 years, Cinda Cree Hicks, and 
his six children, Thomas Ollis Hicks, Jr., Mack Hardin Hicks, John 
Alexander Hicks, Robert Bradley Hicks, William Cree Hicks, and 
Catherine Forgrave Hicks.
  Each of Mr. Hicks' children carries forward his example through their 
own work, families, and contributions to the lives of Americans.
  The House of Representatives recognizes and honors the life, legacy, 
and service of Thomas O. Hicks; recognizes his service to the United 
States as a member of the Army Reserve and the American Battle 
Monuments Commission; acknowledges his lasting contributions to 
American business, sports, education, and civic life, particularly in 
Dallas, Texas; expresses its gratitude for his philanthropy and 
commitment to strengthening communities and creating opportunity 
through job creation and investment; and extends its deepest 
condolences to his wife, Cinda, to his children--Thomas, Mack, Alex, 
Bradley, William, and Catherine--to his grandchildren, and to all who 
mourn his passing.
