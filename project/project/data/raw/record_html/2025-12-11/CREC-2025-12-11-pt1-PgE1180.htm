    HONORING U.S. NAVY PETTY OFFICER THIRD CLASS RONCHESTER MANANGA 
                                SANTIAGO

                                  _____
                                 

                         HON. VICENTE GONZALEZ

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, December 11, 2025

  Mr. VICENTE GONZALEZ of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the 
courage, sacrifice, and service of Mess Management Specialist Third 
Class (MS3) Ronchester M. Santiago, who gave this life in the line of 
duty during the terrorist attack on the USS Cole on October 12, 2000.
  While the USS Cole was refueling at the Port of Aden, Yemen, a 
terrorist detonated an explosive alongside the ship's port side. This 
attack claimed the lives of 17 American sailors, and injured dozens 
more. Among those lost was MS3 Ronchester Santiago, a young man whose 
unwavering dedication to his country and his shipmates exemplified the 
highest ideals of the United States Navy.
  Born and raised in Kingsville, Texas, Petty Officer Santiago embraced 
both service and leadership from a young age. A graduate of H.M. King 
High School and a member of its ROTC program, he answered the call to 
serve soon after earning his high school diploma. His four years in the 
Navy were marked by diligence and integrity.
  Petty Officer Santiago spent three years stationed at the Charlestown 
Navy Yard as a crew member of the USS Constitution, the oldest 
commissioned warship in the world. His dedicated work as a cook and his 
cheerful personality made him an uplifting presence aboard ``Old 
Ironsides,'' and one of the ship's galleys was later named in his 
honor, a lasting tribute to the legacy he left behind.
  Upon joining the crew of the USS Cole, Petty Officer Santiago 
continued to bring professionalism, optimism, and resolve to each 
mission. He took great pride in serving his country and looked forward 
to studying electrical engineering at The University of Texas.
  His service is a testament to the countless young Americans who step 
forward each day to defend our freedoms.
  Mr. Speaker, I wish to express my deepest gratitude to Petty Officer 
Santiago, his family, friends, and all who carry his memory forward. 
May his legacy of courage and dedication continue to inspire future 
generations of Americans.
