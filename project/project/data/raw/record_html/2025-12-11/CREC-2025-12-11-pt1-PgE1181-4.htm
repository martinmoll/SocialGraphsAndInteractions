 PAYING TRIBUTE TO A DEDICATED CONGRESSIONAL STAFF MEMBER, MS. DEBORAH 
                                 PERRY

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. KWEISI MFUME

                              of maryland

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, December 11, 2025

  Mr. MFUME. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to pay tribute to a 
congressional staffer who has played an indispensable role in 
Maryland's 7th Congressional District. She began her well-deserved 
retirement this year after over 29 years of service to the United 
States Congress and the people of Baltimore,
  Ms. Deborah Perry is a Baltimore native, born on December 25, 
Christmas Day, 1950--a birthday that presaged the gift that Ms. Perry 
would become to her community. She grew up with a young U.S. 
Representative Elijah Cummings, beginning a friendship and professional 
relationship that would last decades.
  Ms. Perry graduated from Baltimore's Eastern High School with a 
degree in business education and from the Community College of 
Baltimore with a paralegal degree. She began her career as a civil 
servant, spending nearly a decade working for Baltimore City's Police 
Department and Department of Social Services.
  After briefly working in the Congressional offices of Frederick 
Richmond (D-NY-14) and Julian Dixon (D-CA-28), Ms. Perry started a 
business, Case Management Plus, providing outsourcing services to 
attorneys to assist with personal injury and workers' compensation 
cases.
  In the early 1990's, Ms. Perry began working for Delegate Elijah 
Cummings, then a member of Maryland's House of Delegates. She worked in 
his law office until 1996, when Elijah won the special election to the 
congressional seat that I had vacated. Elijah brought Ms. Perry to 
Congress as his District Office Manager, responsible for training new 
employees and coordinating major projects in the district.
  Over the next 23 years, Ms. Perry performed many roles helping Elijah 
serve the people of Baltimore. After three years as office manager, Ms. 
Perry began assisting constituents resolve their issues with various 
government agencies. She also represented Elijah at important meetings, 
like those for the $1.6 billion East Baltimore Development Project. 
After five years in that role, Ms. Perry became Elijah's Executive 
Assistant, taking on the demanding task of managing his daily schedule.
  After three years as Executive Assistant, Ms. Perry left public 
service in 2007, ready to enjoy retirement. In a testament to her 
character, after the 2008 financial crash, Ms. Perry came out of 
retirement to help handle the influx of housing and foreclosure cases. 
She worked tirelessly for struggling Baltimoreans, developing and 
organizing 12 Foreclosure Prevention Workshops. These workshops were 
well attended, each with more than 1,000 participants. The workshops 
were so successful, she was asked to train other Congressional offices 
on how to host similar workshops in their own districts.
  When she returned to public service in 2009, Ms, Perry intended to 
help for a few months and then return to retirement. For the next 10 
years, she remained an invaluable staffer, serving as Elijah's Special 
Assistant until his untimely death in 2019. When I was elected to 
replace my friend Elijah in my old congressional seat in 2020, I knew 
that Ms. Perry, with decades of experience, would be an asset to my 
office.
  I was fortunate that she agreed to stay in the office as a 
Constituent Services Representative, helping Baltimoreans resolve 
issues with their housing, social services, and utilities. She has 
served with distinction these last 5 years, often speaking of the 
blessing it is to be able to help so many people.
  Ms. Perry's motto is ``Always exceeding the expectations of the 
people she serves in everything she does.'' She has always had a 
passion for helping others, adhering to the Japanese philosophy of 
Kaizen, which encourages change for the better.
  Ms. Deborah Perry has left an indelible mark on Maryland's 7th 
Congressional District. After nearly 30 years of selflessly spreading 
blessings to the people of Baltimore, she is getting the retirement 
that she deserves. Mr. Speaker, I would like to give Ms. Perry the 
recognition that she deserves--recognition of her great 
accomplishments, extensive service, and of the profound impact that she 
has had on our office and the people that we represent. I thank Ms. 
Perry for everything.
