                       HONORING MS. DENYCE GRAVES

                                  _____
                                 

                           HON. KWEISI MFUME

                              of maryland

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, December 11, 2025

  Mr. MFUME. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Ms. Denyce Graves--
world-renowned mezzo-soprano, visionary stage director, and tireless 
advocate for arts education--who has dedicated her career not only to 
performance, but to inspiring, uplifting, and empowering the next 
generation of artists.
  Ms. Graves was raised in Washington, D.C., by her mother, Dorothy 
Graves-Kenner, a fierce believer in education. Dorothy assigned her 
children weekly enrichment projects and nurtured Denyce's early 
affinity for music--first in the church choir, and later in elementary 
school.
  In elementary school, Ms. Graves experienced the transformative power 
of mentorship through her music teacher, Judith Grove, who recognized 
her talent and followed her all the way through high school. She 
attended the Duke Ellington School of the Arts, where she was 
introduced to classical music--and promptly fell in love with the art 
form.
  After high school, Ms. Graves continued her studies at Oberlin 
College and Conservatory and at the New England Conservatory of Music, 
where she honed her vocal technique and deepened her understanding of 
performance.
  Ms. Graves' early career was marked by both triumph and setbacks. She 
impressed at the Metropolitan Opera's regional auditions, but fell 
short in the finals, leading her to take a break from singing and work 
as a hospital secretary. Just as she began to consider leaving music 
behind, the Houston Grand Opera reached out and invited her to their 
young artists program, rekindling her career and setting her on a 
course for the world stage.
  Shortly thereafter, Ms. Graves took the world by storm, captivating 
audiences at the Bastille Opera House in Paris, the Vienna State Opera, 
and other major houses across Europe and the Americas. Her growing 
international acclaim culminated in 1995, with her landmark 
Metropolitan Opera debut in the title role of Carmen. She brought to 
the role a gravitas and a fiery intensity that critics lauded as a 
defining interpretation. She brought that same commanding presence to 
the stage as Dalila in Samson et Dalila, establishing herself as one of 
the great operatic superstars of the modern era.
  Throughout her career, Ms. Graves has been honored repeatedly for her 
artistic achievements and philanthropic leadership. She has won an Emmy 
and a Grammy, as well as the Washington Performing Arts' Ambassador of 
the Arts Award, She was named a U.S. Cultural Ambassador, sang at the 
National Prayer Service following the September 11, 2001, terrorist 
attacks, and performed at President George W. Bush's second 
inauguration. Most recently, she received the Service to America 
Leadership Award from the National Association of Broadcasters 
Leadership Foundation, in recognition of her unwavering dedication to 
public service through the arts.
  These honors reflect not only Ms. Graves' extraordinary talent, but 
also her commitment to using her talent in service of others. She is a 
teacher and mentor at the Juilliard School, the Peabody Conservatory, 
and the University of Toronto. She founded the Denyce Graves Foundation 
in 2021, with a two-pronged mission: to advance diversity in her field 
and to honor the legacies of historically overlooked voices. Their 
``Hidden Voices'' program highlights and celebrates the artistic 
contributions of historically marginalized musicians, composers, and 
educators. Their flagship ``Shared Voices'' initiative connects 
Historically Black Colleges and Universities with top music 
conservatories, expanding access and fostering inclusion for the next 
generation of artists.
  Mr. Speaker, the remarkable career of Ms. Denyce Graves reflects a 
rare combination of artistic excellence and passionate leadership in 
education. Through her performances, advocacy, and mentorship, she has 
opened doors for countless artists and enriched the cultural fabric of 
our Nation. I am proud to celebrate this visionary artist whose story 
will inspire generations to come.
