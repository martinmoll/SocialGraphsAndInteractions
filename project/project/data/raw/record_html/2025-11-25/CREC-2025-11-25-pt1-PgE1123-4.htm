                      HONORING LEONA COOPER-BAKER

                                  _____
                                 

                        HON. FREDERICA S. WILSON

                               of florida

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 25, 2025

  Ms. WILSON of Florida. Mr. Speaker, from the 24th district of the 
great State of Florida, I rise today to honor the life and legacy of 
Leona Cooper-Baker, a lifelong resident of the City of Miami's Coconut 
Grove neighborhood. I include in the Record the following Proclamation 
honoring Leona Cooper-Baker.
       Whereas, Leona Louise Cooper was born on December 26, 1936. 
     She is a proud descendant of Miami's original Bahamian 
     settlers, and her life was a testament to the rich history 
     and enduring legacy of Miami's newly designated Little 
     Bahamas of Coconut Grove neighborhood; and
       Whereas, she was one of nine children born to parents who 
     migrated to Miami from Eleuthera, Bahamas, and she lived her 
     entire life in the City of Coral Gables' Golden Gate 
     subdivision, known for their historic Bahamian bungalow and 
     shotgun wood frame style houses, in a home built on the land 
     where her parents first settled; and
       Whereas, after graduating from George Washington Carver 
     School in 1954, Mrs. Cooper-Baker pursued higher education at 
     Lincoln University of Missouri, earning a degree in 
     Elementary Education in 1960; and
       Whereas, Mrs. Cooper-Baker dedicated over three decades of 
     her life to educating the youth of Miami-Dade County, 
     beginning her career at Rainbow Park Elementary School in 
     Opa-Locka, continuing at Bunche Park and George Washington 
     Carver, and concluding her distinguished service, in 1992 at 
     Shenandoah Elementary, where she retired; and
       Whereas, affectionately known as the ``Coconut Grove 
     Historian,'' Mrs. Cooper-Baker was a passionate advocate for 
     the preservation of Black and Bahamian history in Coconut 
     Grove and Coral Gables, compiling an extensive archive of 
     historic photographs, books, and articles that document the 
     rich cultural heritage of the community; and
       Whereas, Mrs. Cooper-Baker's contributions to historic 
     preservation extended to her leadership roles with the Miami/
     Bahamas Goombay Festival's Historical Committee, the 
     Historical Museum of Southern Florida, the Coral Gables 
     Museum, and the Black Archives, ensuring that the stories of 
     Coconut Grove's pioneers would be preserved for future 
     generations; and
       Whereas, Mrs. Cooper-Baker played an instrumental role in 
     securing the designation of Coral Gables MacFarlane Homestead 
     Historic District on the National Register of Historic Places 
     and served as a member of the Coral Gables Historic 
     Preservation Board; and
       Whereas, her tireless work to preserve the history of the 
     Little Bahamas neighborhood,

[[Page E1124]]

     Golden Gate subdivision, and the broader Coconut Grove 
     community has left an indelible mark, earning her widespread 
     respect and admiration; and
       Whereas, Mrs. Cooper-Baker was preceded in death by her 
     parents and siblings, and she is survived by numerous 
     cousins, nieces, nephews, and other relatives, including her 
     beloved niece Clarice Cooper, president of the Coconut Grove 
     Village West Homeowners and Tenants Association; and
       Whereas, the Coconut Grove community and all who were 
     touched by her life will forever cherish the memory of her 
     generosity, wisdom, and unwavering dedication to the 
     preservation of history and education; and
       Now, therefore, be it resolved that I, Frederica S. Wilson, 
     a member of the United States House of Representatives 
     representing the 24th Congressional District of Florida, am 
     honored to recognize the life of Leona Louise Cooper-Baker, 
     extend our deepest condolences to her family, friends, and 
     loved ones, and honor her immeasurable contributions to the 
     community and to the preservation of Black and Bahamian 
     history and culture.
