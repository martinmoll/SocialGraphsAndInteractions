                         REMEMBERING JOHN GARZA

                                  _____
                                 

                           HON. GREG STANTON

                               of arizona

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 25, 2025

  Mr. STANTON. Mr. Speaker, I rise to remember John Garza, a dedicated 
Tempe firefighter, Marine Corps veteran, and lifelong public servant 
who passed away earlier this

[[Page E1120]]

month after a courageous battle with cancer. John lived a full life 
with a deep commitment to service.
  John served with Tempe Fire Medical Rescue for twenty years. He 
responded to countless emergencies and delivered lifesaving care to 
thousands of Arizonans. He served several roles, from paramedic to 
captain, to engineer, and took pride in mentoring younger firefighters. 
Colleagues remember his solid judgment, quick thinking, and a steady 
presence he brought to even the most difficult calls. His last 
assignment, Engine 273 at Station 3, was a place where he earned the 
respect and friendship of everyone he served with.
  John's dedication to the people of Tempe extended far beyond the 
calls he answered. A proud graduate of Tempe High School, he knew the 
neighborhoods, families, and history of this community as well as 
anyone. Fellow firefighters describe the warmth he brought into every 
room, the humor that lifted spirits on hard days, and the genuine 
kindness that made people feel seen and appreciated. He was the kind of 
public servant who led by example, placing others first and treating 
every person with respect and dignity.
  Love of country and a belief in service shaped his life well before 
joining the fire department. As a United States Marine, John embodied 
courage, discipline, and devotion to country. He brought those values 
with him throughout his life and showed tremendous work ethic, passion, 
and pride in helping others. He possessed those same traits as a 
husband, father, and friend. Outside the firehouse, he found joy in the 
outdoors, whether hunting, fishing, camping, cooking, or golfing with 
the people he loved.
  John is survived by his wife, Liana; his children, John Jr. and Maya; 
his parents, Robert and Nora; his sister, Elizabeth, and her children, 
PJ and Brooke. He leaves behind extended family, fellow Marines, and 
generations of firefighters who admire his character and carry his 
example into their own service.
  Tempe is a better place because of John. He saved lives and 
selflessly uplifted those who had the privilege of knowing him.
  Mr. Speaker, on behalf of the people of Arizona, I extend my deepest 
condolences to his family and ask my colleagues to join me in honoring 
a firefighter, veteran, husband, father, and friend whose life reflects 
the very best of public service.
