   REMEMBERING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF THE HONORABLE NATHAN SUBER, III

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                         Monday, June 23, 2025

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, it is with a heavy heart and fond 
remembrance that I rise today to honor a dedicated community leader, 
faithful servant of God and dear friend of long standing, The Honorable 
Nathan Suber, III. Sadly, Nathan transitioned from labor to reward on 
June 16, 2025. A Homegoing Celebration of his remarkable life will be 
held at 11:00 a.m. on Tuesday, June 24, 2025, at the Fourth Street 
Missionary Baptist Church, in Columbus, Georgia.
  The genesis of Nathan Suber's life occurred on July 20, 1950, in 
Phenix City, Alabama with his birth to the late Staff Sergeant (Ret.) 
Nathan Suber, Jr. and Helen Woods Douthit. In 1968, Nathan graduated 
from South Girard High School in Phenix City and furthered his 
education with an Associate Degree from City College of San Francisco 
and a Bachelor's of Science Degree from Columbus College, now Columbus 
State University, in Georgia.
  Nathan began his career journey in law enforcement as a Patrol 
Officer with the Police

[[Page E600]]

Department of the Consolidated Government of Columbus Georgia. He 
advanced to the rank of Detective. Serving and protecting the people of 
Columbus, Nathan was part of the incredible team that investigated and 
resolved the infamous ``Stocking Strangler'' serial murder cases that 
terrorized the city of Columbus for more than 6 months in the late 
1970's. His career path then led him to the private sector where he 
excelled as a Sales Manager for Bell South Yellow Pages, and Davis 
Broadcasting, Inc., before becoming Sales Representative for AFLAC 
Insurance. He concluded his professional career as Manager for Krispy 
Kreme, under 755 Restaurant Corporation, a family business owned by his 
uncle and aunt, Henry ''Hank'' Aaron and Billye Suber Aaron, icons in 
American sports, business, education and philanthropy. Nathan gave good 
account for his stewardship in all his career endeavors.
  Beyond his career achievements, Nathan was a pillar of strength in 
his community, serving 12 years as District 1 Councilor for the 
Consolidated Government of Columbus Georgia, including as Chairperson 
of the City Council Budget Committee. Moreover, Nathan served as Board 
Chair of the Metro Columbus Urban League and held positions with the A. 
J. McClung YMCA, Columbus Board Initiative and the Midtown Project, 
demonstrating his enduring commitment to bettering his community. It 
has been said that ``Service is the rent we pay for the space we occupy 
on this earth.'' Nathan Suber paid his rent and paid it well.
  Nathan Suber was a man of deep faith and a servant of God. For more 
than 50 years he served God through the ministries of the Fourth Street 
Missionary Baptist Church. For 20 of those years at Fourth Street, 
Nathan and I were in the same Sunday School class and sang and traveled 
together with the Inspirational and Mass Choirs. Nathan went on to 
serve as Superintendent of Sunday School and Chair of the Trustee 
Ministry. He was truly a pillar of the church.
  On a personal note, Nathan was my friend for more than half a 
century. He supported me in every endeavor I took in public life and 
undertook on many roles that proved vital to my success. And, like the 
Biblical Nathan who counseled King David, Suber and I counseled each 
other over the years through good times and bad, offering our best 
advice to one another on personal, public, and confidential matters. 
Nathan was brutally honest, telling me not what I wanted to hear but 
what he felt I needed to hear. I will always be grateful for the 
fidelity of his friendship and the timbre of his character.
  Nathan accomplished much in his life, but none of it would have been 
possible without the grace of God and the love and support of his 
devoted wife of 48 years, Charlene Brown Suber; his two daughters, 
Kelley Suber Jones and Cynthia Denise Suber Huffman; his three 
cherished grandchildren, Morgan, Austin, and Charleston; his sister, 
Artie Suber Cobb and brother, Michael Suber; as well as his aunts, 
uncle, nieces, nephews and other extended family.
  As we reflect on Nathan's life, we honor his unwavering faith, his 
tireless commitment to serving others and his passion for making his 
community better. Columbus, Georgia, our state, our Nation, and the 
world have been enriched by the contributions of the Honorable Nathan 
Suber, III.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues, to join my wife, Vivian, and me, 
along with the more than 765,000 people of Georgia's 2nd Congressional 
District in honoring the life and legacy of the Honorable Nathan Suber, 
III and in extending our deepest condolences to his family, friends, 
and all who mourn his loss. Moreover, we pray that we will all be 
consoled and comforted by an abiding faith and the Holy Spirit in the 
days, weeks and months ahead.
