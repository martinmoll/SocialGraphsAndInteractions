           HONORING COLONEL VU VAN LPC'S LIFETIME OF SERVICE

                                  _____
                                 

                          HON. SAM T. LICCARDO

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, December 17, 2025

  Mr. LICCARDO. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the life and 
contributions of Colonel Vu Van Lrc, a South Vietnamese Army veteran 
and beloved community leader in San Jose and the greater Silicon Valley 
of California, for his unwavering dedication to Vietnamese refugees and 
cultural perservation--a true model of resilience and service.
  From the rice fields of Nam Dinh to the heart of Silicon Valley, 
Colonel Lrc's journey was extraordinary. Born in 1933 in northern 
Vietnam, he graduated from the Da Lat Military Academy and rose through 
the ranks to serve as a Colonel in the Army of the Republic of Vietnam. 
When Saigon fell on April 30, 1975, Colonel Lrc and his family were 
forced to flee their homeland, ultimately finding refuge in San Jose, 
California. Rather than succumb to the tragedy of exile, he transformed 
it into a new mission of hope.
  In 1976, soon after arriving in San Jose, Colonel Lrc founded the 
Immigrant Resettlement & Cultural Center (IRCC) to aid fellow refugees 
in rebuilding their lives. Over the next four decades, the IRCC became 
a lifeline for thousands of Vietnamese, Cambodian, and Laotian 
families. Colonel Lrc and his team provided English classes, job 
training, housing assistance, and citizenship guidance to more than 
20,000 refugees in Northern California. Under his leadership, the 
center also fostered cultural and civic events like the annual TIt 
Lunar New Year festivals, helping new immigrant preserve their 
cherished traditions as they integrated into American life.
  In addition to addressing immediate resettlement needs, Colonel Lrc 
then started preserving the historical heritage of his people. He began 
collecting donated mementos that reflected the diverse experiences of 
Vietnamese refugees, from veterans who had fought alongside him, to 
boat people who survived harrowing ocean escapes, to those who endured 
brutal ``re-education'' camps after the war. Over three decades, 
Colonel Lrc assembled these artifacts and stories, determined to create 
an enduring home for the history of the Vietnamese diaspora. That 
vision culminated in the establishment of the Museum of the Boat People 
and the Republic of Vietnam, better known simply as the Vist Museum, 
which opened in San Jose's History Park in 2007. Housed in a restored 
historic building, the museum contains hundreds of precious artifacts, 
photographs, and works of art that tell the story of American journey. 
The museum would not have come together without immense personal 
sacrifice from Colonel Lrc who even mortgaged his family home to 
sustain the museum during difficult times. Thanks to his dedication, 
the Vist Museum stands today as a treasure of San Jose, educating 
future generations about the courage, sacrifice, and contributions of 
Vietnamese refugees.
  Colonel Vu Van Lrc passed away peacefully on November 29, 2025, at 
the age of 92, surrounded by his loved ones and honored by a grateful 
community. For all of his military rank and community titles, he most 
cherished his role as a loving husband, father, and grandfather. While 
Colonel Lrc's journey began in war and exile, it became our community 
great fortune that he made his second home in San Jose, and that we 
could come to know Colonel Vu Van Lrc as a friend, mentor, and leader. 
Today, we honor his memory, and his legacy, which lives on in the 
hearts of more than 20,000 immigrant Americans who he supported in 
their brutally difficult journey, and whose journey itself has become 
an inspiration for our Nation.
