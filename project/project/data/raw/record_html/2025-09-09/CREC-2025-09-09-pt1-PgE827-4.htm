                HONORING THE SERVICE OF JOHN B. ROBINSON

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. CHRIS PAPPAS

                            of new hampshire

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, September 9, 2025

  Mr. PAPPAS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in recognition of John B. 
Robinson, a constituent from Portsmouth, New Hampshire. I celebrate 
John's service to our country as the leader of the 5th U.S. Marine 
Division Special Rangers during the pivotal World War II battle of Iwo 
Jima.
  John was just eighteen years old when Japan bombed Pearl Harbor. He 
enlisted in the Marines after high school and soon found himself a 
Staff Sergeant in charge of a sound ranging team that specialized in 
locating hidden enemy weapons.
  John's sound rangers took part in the Marine landing on the small 
volcanic island of Iwo Jima in February 1945. Upon landing at Iwo Jima, 
the Marines faced a surprise Japanese assault. Enemy fighters streamed 
out of underground tunnels and launched attacks with hidden artillery 
positioned across the island. Nearly seven thousand Marines were 
killed. But were it not for the work of John and his team, countless 
more American lives might have been lost.
  In the thirty-six days of fighting that ensued, John and the 5th 
Marine Division's sound rangers used cutting-edge technology to 
echolocate Japanese weapons before they could be used against American 
troops. They persisted through a constant barrage of enemy fire that 
kept them awake day and night. Many saw fellow Marines die right before 
their eyes. John later recalled that ``Every time [he] took a breath, 
someone got killed'' on Iwo Jima, including many of his close personal 
friends.
  At 102 years of age, John is the only surviving member of the 5th 
Division sound rangers. He marked his birthday this April by sharing 
war stories with nearly 100 community members gathered at his 
Portsmouth assisted living facility. John has also documented his 
service in Iwo Jima and postwar occupied Japan by donating oral history 
recordings to the Story Preservation Initiative.
  On behalf of New Hampshire's 1st Congressional District, I would like 
to thank John for his service and for sharing his wartime experiences 
with others. As we reflect on the 80th anniversary of V-J Day this 
month, it is more important than ever to listen to veterans like 
yourself who fought with ingenuity and courage when our democracy was 
on the line.
