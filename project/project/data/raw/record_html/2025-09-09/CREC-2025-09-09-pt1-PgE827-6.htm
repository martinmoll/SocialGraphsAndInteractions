             HONORING THE LIFE AND SERVICE OF ANDREW STINE

                                 ______
                                 

                         HON. ELISE M. STEFANIK

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, September 9, 2025

  Ms. STEFANIK. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the incredible life 
and service of United States Army Veteran, Andrew G. Stine.

[[Page E828]]

  Andrew was born on June 22, 1923, in Saratoga Springs, New York, and 
graduated from White Plains High School in 1941. He briefly attended 
New York University before entering the United States Army on March 4, 
1943, at the height of World War II. Andrew was assigned as a 
Demolition Tech with the 1884th Engineer Aviation Battalion, with whom 
he served in the Ryukyus campaign, a pivotal campaign in the Pacific 
Theater, which included the Battle of Okinawa. Andrew and his unit 
handled the construction and repair of airfields in remote and hostile 
areas throughout the Western Pacific. After being honorably discharged 
at the end of World War II, Andrew opted to re-enlist with the Army 
Corps of Engineers on February 21, 1947, and served as a surveyor with 
the 817th Engineer Aviation Battalion for another three years.
  On February 20, 1950, Andrew was honorably discharged, and in 
recognition of his dedicated service to the United States, he was 
presented with the Good Conduct Medal with Clasp, the Asiatic-Pacific 
Campaign Medal with two Service Stars, the American Campaign Medal, and 
the World War II Victory Medal.
  After his military service, Andrew went on to receive his bachelor's 
degree from Skidmore College and worked as a land surveyor for over 
fifty years, including ten years working for the National Park Service, 
and another eight years with the Vermont Highway and Human Services 
Department.
  Andrew and his wife, Ethel, were married for fifty years and raised 
three children together: Patricia, Marion, and James. They were also 
heavily involved with Boy Scout Troop No. 13 in Schuylerville, New 
York, and were proud to see their son and grandson both attain the rank 
of Eagle Scout. On July 4, 2019, Andrew passed away, leaving behind a 
legacy of service to his country and his community.
  On behalf of New York's 21st Congressional District, I am honored to 
recognize World War II Veteran Andrew G. Stine.
