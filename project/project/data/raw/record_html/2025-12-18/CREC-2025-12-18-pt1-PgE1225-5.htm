    RECOGNIZING THE MEMBERS OF THE EMERGENCY MEDICAL SERVICES, EMS 
                               PROFESSION

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. BRAD FINSTAD

                              of minnesota

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, December 18, 2025

  Mr. FINSTAD. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the members of 
the emergency

[[Page E1226]]

medical services (EMS) profession who dedicate their lives to providing 
often lifesaving medical care and transport services to patients as 
well as entire communities. These health care professionals are on the 
frontlines of providing vital emergency 9-1-1 and interfacility 
ambulance services.
  Every year, the dispatch of an ambulance is the first response to 
millions of medical emergencies. Often, the survival of a patient is 
enhanced by the prompt medical attention provided by paramedics and 
emergency medical technicians (EMTs) prior to the arrival at an 
emergency department. As a result of the selfless acts of these 
courageous and devoted professions, the lives of thousands of Americans 
are saved each year. While these professionals do not expect to receive 
recognition for their work, they deserve our outmost gratitude.
  I would like to highlight a very special group of EMS professionals 
or ``Stars of Life'' who were recently honored by the American 
Ambulance Association. This year, the American Ambulance Association is 
recognizing 147 Stars of Life--by far the largest number of Stars of 
Life in the over 25 years of the program.
  The American Ambulance Association honors each year those paramedics, 
emergency medical technicians, dispatchers and other ambulance service 
personnel who exemplify what is best about the EMS field. The American 
Ambulance Association has appropriately designated these individuals as 
``Stars of Life''. Past Stars of Life have included paramedics and EMTs 
who were part of evacuation and response efforts to natural disasters, 
acts of terrorism and public health emergencies. Members of this year's 
Stars of Life program are no different and were involved with 
exceptional acts such as stabilizing an expecting mothers in medical 
distress and saving the life of both the mother and newborn baby and 
using quick thinking and their medical skills to save individuals 
involved in vehicle crashes.
  Of equal importance, this program also pays tribute to those 
ambulance service personnel whose heroic acts or community service 
activities may not have made the news, but were just as meaningful to 
the people they aided. They are truly America's health care safety net.
  I am especially proud of the four Stars of Life from Minnesota being 
recognized this year who serve Minnesota with pride. They are John 
Harding, Stacy Jensen, Daniel McIntosh and Daniel Rohe. I had the 
opportunity to meet with John Harding with Mayo Clinic Ambulance from 
North Mankato, Minnesota and can tell you he is a first-class EMS 
professional who deeply cares about his community and his patients.
