                              NO NEW WARS

  (Mr. QUIGLEY asked and was given permission to address the House for 
1 minute and to revise and extend his remarks.)
  Mr. QUIGLEY. Mr. Speaker, in 1848, a freshman Congressman from 
Illinois wrote that the Constitution gives war-making powers to 
Congress, and the ability of one man to initiate a war places our 
President where kings have always stood. That was Abraham Lincoln.
  Today, the American people do not want another bloody, endless war, 
but a President who campaigned on no new wars is dragging this country 
and our allies into a war with little explanation and no clear long-
term strategy.
  The President has bluntly stated that the deaths of American 
servicemembers are a price he is willing to pay. Yet, just 6 months 
ago, he insisted that Iran's nuclear program had been completely and 
totally obliterated.
  If that was the case, then what is the rationale for starting this 
war?
  Our men and women in uniform are not disposable. Americans want no 
part of this war.
