           HONORING THE LIFE OF REVEREND JAMES D. PETERS, JR.

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. DIANA DeGETTE

                              of colorado

                    in the house of representatives

                         Thursday, May 15, 2025

  Ms. DeGETTE. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and commemorate the 
life of Reverend James D. Peters, Jr., a titan of civil rights, a moral 
leader in Denver's faith community, and a man who dedicated his life to 
lifting up the voices and needs of the most vulnerable among us. 
Reverend Peters leaves behind a powerful legacy of faith in action, 
tireless advocacy, and an unwavering commitment to Justice.
  Born in an era marked by deep segregation and inequality, Reverend 
Peters found his calling at the intersection of ministry and the Civil 
Rights Movement. He marched alongside Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. in 
Selma, Alabama, crossing the Edmund Pettus Bridge in 1965 in the fight 
for voting rights. He was present at the founding meeting of the 
Southern Christian Leadership Conference. But as those closest to him 
will attest, Reverend Peters did not merely honor history--he made it, 
lived it, and carried its mission forward in Denver.
  Reverend Peters moved to Denver in 1978 to serve as the pastor of New 
Hope Baptist Church--a position he held with distinction for nearly 
three decades. Through this ministry, he became a spiritual anchor, 
civil rights leader, and moral conscience for the city. Though he was 
never a candidate for public office, he helped shape public life in 
Colorado. He chaired the Colorado Civil Rights Commission and served as 
a trusted voice within the Colorado Democratic Party, always pushing 
for a broader, more inclusive vision of Justice.
  Reverend Peters was truly a man of deep conscience and unwavering 
resolve. He stood up for what was right--even when it was not popular--
and he never lost the fire that fueled the Civil Rights Movement. He 
always stood firmly with community leaders demanding justice and 
systemic reform. His presence gave strength to all Denverites and lent 
moral clarity to those fighting for change.
  His advocacy extended far beyond politics and deep into people's 
lives. Reverend Peters inspired countless elected leaders, including 
State Senator Rhonda Fields, whom he encouraged to run for office after 
the tragic loss of her son. On a personal note, Reverend Peters was a 
friend and confidante of mine for many years, generously sharing his 
wisdom and advice.
  Reverend Peters was not only a champion for civil rights, he was also 
a unifier. As his ministry reached across the racial divides, he 
reminded us that faith calls us not only to believe, but to act, not 
only to pray, but to serve, not only to hope, but to build.
  He loved the Rockies. He loved the Broncos. He loved his friends. And 
above all, he loved his church and community.
  Today, Mr. Speaker, we honor the life of Reverend James D. Peters, 
Jr., a man who walked with history, who shepherded a city, and who 
never ceased to care for the people of Denver and beyond. May we carry 
forward his vision of justice, compassion, and unity.
