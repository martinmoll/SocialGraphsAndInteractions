     HONORING THE LIFE OF MR. SCHLEY LOUIS ``BIG LOUIE'' PERRY, JR.

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, December 1, 2025

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and 
remarkable service of a loving husband, dedicated father, doting 
grandfather, ingenious advocate for agriculture, and my longtime friend 
and steadfast supporter, Schley Louis ``Big Louie'' Perry, Jr., who 
passed away on November 26, 2025, at the age of 86. A memorial service 
to celebrate his remarkable life will be held on Monday, December 1, 
2025, at the Baker Funeral Home in Moultrie, Georgia.
  Born on March 10, 1939, on Pineywoods Farms in Colquitt County, to 
the union of Schley Louis Perry, Jr. and Lois Perry, Big Louie was the 
fifth generation to steward the family land. Pineywoods Farms, 
established in 1830, is a Centennial Farm and the oldest continually 
owned business in the county--a legacy Louie treasured, protected and 
preserved. Except for his college years, Louie lived and worked on the 
farm his entire life, and today his children and grandchildren continue 
that tradition as the sixth and seventh generations.
  Louie's devotion to agriculture began early. He joined the Georgia 
Farm Bureau at 13, was an active FFA member, and earned an associate's 
degree in agriculture from ABAC in 1959 before studying Agribusiness at 
the University of Georgia. Returning to Pineywoods Farms in 1962, he 
modernized operations, expanded production, and partnered with UGA 
Extension on on-farm research. An early adopter of center-pivot and 
variable-rate irrigation, Louie transitioned to a nationally recognized 
Limousin seedstock operation and marketed cattle nationwide. His 
commitment to innovation and stewardship made him a leader among 
Georgia producers.
  Big Louie believed deeply in service. To him, farming was more than a 
business--he considered it a duty to the land and the next generation, 
a sentiment that guided his life. His advocacy advanced producer-driven 
programs, policy, and markets; he counseled elected officials to 
include me, Former President Jimmy Carter, Former United States Senator 
Saxby Chambliss, Former United States Senator Johnny Isakson, and 
former Governors Roy Barnes and Sonny Perdue, hosted farm tours, and 
mentored emerging leaders. As I have often observed, ``Service is the 
rent that we pay for the space that we occupy here on this earth'' and 
Louie embodied that truth in every role he held.
  His leadership spanned commodity boards and associations--including 
three decades on the Georgia Cotton Commission (Chair, 2000 to 2012), 
Cotton, Inc. (Chair, 2006), the National Cotton Council, Georgia and 
regional Limousin organizations, and more. He served 19 years on the 
Colquitt County Commission under the slogan ``Young, Able and Willing 
to Serve,'' and contributed to health care, education, and civic life 
through the Colquitt Regional Medical Center Foundation, the Pineland 
School Board, the Chamber of Commerce, Kiwanis Club and his church.
  Honors reflecting his lifetime of service include the Georgia Cotton 
Commission Inaugural Trustees Award (2023), ABAC Family Legacy Award 
(2022), Centennial Family Farm Award (2020), and the J. Lamar Branch 
Leadership in Agriculture Award (2020).
  Louie created the Pineywoods Farms Scholarship at UGA in 2007 and 
endowed scholarships at ABAC and UGA to support future agricultural 
leaders. He leaves a legacy of mentorship, innovation, and civic 
responsibility.
  On a personal note, Big Louie was my friend and one of my biggest 
supporters. His support and loyalty were unquestioned, and he was 
always brutally honest in his advice and counsel to me. I will be 
forever grateful for the fidelity of his friendship. The great 
agricultural chemist George Washington Carver once said that ``How far 
you go in life depends on your being tender with young, compassionate 
with the aged, sympathetic with striving and tolerant of the weak and 
the strong, because some day in your life you will have been all of 
these.'' Big Louie went far in life because he treated all people with 
dignity and respect, and he followed God's purpose for his life.
  He is survived by his wife of 61 years, Rebecca Clark Perry; children 
Laura Perry Johnson (Scott), Schley Louis Perry, III (Lisa), and 
Jonathan Johnson Perry (Jackie); and grandchildren Bill and Libba 
Johnson, Jack and Alex Perry, and Zach and Alli Perry.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join my wife, Vivian, and I, 
along with the more than 765,000 people of Georgia's 2nd Congressional 
District in honoring the life and legacy of Schley Louis ``Big Louie'' 
Perry, Jr. He lived a life defined by duty to family, land, and 
community. His work fed our tables, his leadership strengthened the 
agriculture industry, and his service lifted our people. Moreover, we 
extend our deepest sympathies to his family, friends, loved ones and 
all those who grieve during this difficult time of bereavement. We pray 
that they will all be consoled and comforted by an abiding faith and 
the Holy Spirit in the days, weeks, and months ahead.
