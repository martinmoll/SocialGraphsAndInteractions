  HONORING PRIVATE FIRST CLASS JESUS ANTONIO PENA, UNITED STATES ARMY

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. TONY GONZALES

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, December 15, 2025

  Mr. TONY GONZALES of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a man 
whose legacy is both profoundly American and profoundly personal to 
me--my grandfather,

[[Page E1201]]

Jesus Antonio Pena, a soldier, a father, a civil servant, and an 
enduring example of quiet courage.
  Born to Mrs. Luisa G. Pena and Mr. Jesus G. Pena, my grandfather grew 
up in Texas, in a home where service, sacrifice, and faith were part of 
everyday life. At just 18 years old, he was drafted into the United 
States Army on December 21, 1943. Like so many young men of his 
generation, he answered his country's call without hesitation, stepping 
into history during one of the most dangerous moments the world has 
ever faced.
  After basic training at Fort Sam Houston and Camp Shelby, 
Mississippi, he deployed overseas on August 4, 1944. Soon, found 
himself on the shores of Utah Beach, landing in the shadow of D-Day and 
entering into the heart of World War II. From there, he fought through 
the freezing forests and unimaginable hardship of the Battle of the 
Bulge, one of the bloodiest and most pivotal engagements of the war.
  Mr. Speaker, my grandfather was not alone in his service. He was one 
of six sons, four of them soldiers, all of them proud sons of South 
Texas, and he now lays to rest together at Fort Sam Houston National 
Cemetery. Our family's sacrifice is etched into the soil of that 
hallowed ground and into the history of our Nation.
  One story, passed down through generations, speaks to both his 
humility and his conviction. When he reported for induction, the Army 
had mistakenly stamped his paperwork for the Navy. My grandfather 
looked at the officer and said, ``Sir, all my brothers are in the 
Army.'' Without hesitation, the officer took the form, stamped Army 
across it, and told him, ``Alright then, get out of here.'' And with 
that, he joined his brothers in service.
  After the war, he continued serving his country, this time in 
civilian life. For 35 years, he worked at Kelly Air Force Base as an 
airplane electrician, ensuring that generations of service members had 
safe and reliable aircraft. He was a man who took pride in his work, 
who believed in doing things right, and who understood that service did 
not end when the uniform came off.
  He returned home to his wife, Ruth, and together they built a life 
rooted in love, family, and perseverance. His eldest son was named in 
his honor, carrying forward his name and his legacy.
  He was the embodiment of the values that define our greatest 
generation: duty, honor, sacrifice, and unwavering devotion to country. 
He was my greatest role model and inspired me to become a Master Chief 
Petty Officer of the Navy.
  Mr. Speaker, it is one of the greatest honors of my life to stand in 
this Chamber and speak his name: Jesus Antonio Pena. A young man from 
Texas who journeyed across an ocean to defend liberty. A soldier who 
braved the snows of Belgium. A patriot who came home and continued to 
serve. A grandfather whose strength and humility continue to guide his 
family to this day.
