   HONORING THE 50TH WEDDING ANNIVERSARY OF DEBRA AND EDWARD SULLIVAN

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JERROLD NADLER

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, December 9, 2025

  Mr. NADLER. Mr. Speaker, today I rise to mark 50 years of enduring 
marriage between Debra and Edward Sullivan. High school sweethearts who 
married on September 27, 1975, at St. Joseph's Church in Babylon, New 
York. Today, we honor a love that only a very few are fortunate enough 
to celebrate in their lifetime.
  Edward, or Eddie as all know him, son of Florence and John Sullivan, 
and Debra, or Debbie, daughter of Dolores and Ronald Sup, have together 
built a life grounded in love, patience, and respect.
  From Eddie's early beginnings as a clammer on the Great South Bay, 
together they have shared a lifetime of memories that serve as a model 
for all of us to follow.
  Proud parents to Dr. Lauren Sullivan Goddard, and grandparents to 
Gavin and Reese Goddard, the Sullivans have always put family first in 
their lives. Godparents to Matthew Jansen, they have always served as 
mentors and the glue that keeps their family together and their 
traditions alive.
  Never far from the ocean, the Sullivans' shared love of boating and 
the sunshine has always kept them active and enjoying all the South 
Shore of Long Island has to offer.
  While many days can seem routine--dinner at 5, dog walks with 
Debbie's sister Lynn at 6, Jeopardy at 7 followed by Wheel of Fortune--
they have always found ways to keep their bond fresh and new. From 
vacations to the Florida Keys to Montana, Alaska, and beyond, they keep 
the adventure going.
  Mr. Speaker, if you were to crack open a dictionary and find the 
``American Dream,'' you would find Debbie and Eddie Sullivan. For 
decades, Eddie worked tirelessly as a union carpenter and Debbie as a 
supervisor at Marchon Eyewear--careers that allowed them to build a 
beautiful home in their shared hometown of West Islip, New York. 
Together, they put their pride and joy, Lauren, through college and 
pharmacy school to achieve her doctorate, a milestone that is 
celebrated universally throughout their family.
  Through it all, Debbie and Eddie have upheld their vows to care for 
each other through sickness and health. No matter the circumstances, 
they always stand by each other. The Sullivans have survived many 
trials, but their unwavering care and commitment have defined their 50 
years together.
  Mr. Speaker, we all could learn something from Deb and Ed Sullivan. 
They are truly upstanding New Yorkers who carry themselves with the 
spirit of hard work and pride, and who have perfected this commitment 
to each other. They are truly blessed to have a strong marriage and 
family.
  At a time when our Nation is so fraught and divided, it is comforting 
to know that we have neighbors like the Sullivans among us. They are 
the best America has to offer, and I ask all my colleagues to join me 
in congratulating the Sullivans on this momentous achievement and in 
wishing them many more years of health and happiness.
