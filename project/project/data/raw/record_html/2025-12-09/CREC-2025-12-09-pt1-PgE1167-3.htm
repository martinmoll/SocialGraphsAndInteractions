HONORING TULLYTOWN BOROUGH MAYOR DAVID CUTCHINEAL'S RETIREMENT AFTER 4 
                           DECADES OF SERVICE

                                  _____
                                 

                       HON. BRIAN K. FITZPATRICK

                            of pennsylvania

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, December 9, 2025

  Mr. FITZPATRICK. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and recognize an 
extraordinary public servant, United States Navy Veteran and longtime 
Mayor of Tullytown Borough, David ``Dave'' Cutchineal, on the occasion 
of his retirement, following more than 33 years as Mayor and over 40 
years of dedicated service to his beloved community in Bucks County, 
Pennsylvania.
  Mayor Cutchineal's relationship with Tullytown is rooted in 
generations of commitment to civic leadership. His family name is woven 
deeply into the fabric of the borough's history. His father, Joseph 
Cutchineal, Jr., served 18 years on borough council, and his 
grandfather, Joseph Sr., served 12 years before him. Continuing this 
proud legacy of public service, Mayor Cutchineal first joined the 
Tullytown Borough Council in 1983, serving until 1988. This included as 
Council President and later returning from 1994 to 1998. In 1988, he 
was elected Mayor of Tullytown, serving continuously, with the 
exception of his second tenure on council, until today. Across more 
than three decades, Mayor Cutchineal has provided steady leadership, 
strong advocacy for local families, and an unwavering devotion to the 
community he calls home.
  Before entering government, Mayor Cutchineal answered a greater call 
to service. From 1969 to 1973, he served honorably in the United States 
Navy as a SATCOM Radioman 3rd Class Petty Officer, maintaining Compass 
Link communications between Vietnam and the Pentagon during a critical 
period of war. For his service, he was awarded the Naval Unit 
Commendation Medal. A proud veteran, Mayor Cutchineal has never stopped 
honoring those who serve. He has organized Tullytown's annual Memorial 
Day Service for many years, ensuring that the sacrifices of America's 
fallen heroes are remembered with dignity.
  Outside of elected office, Mayor Cutchineal built a distinguished 
civilian career with Bucks County Consumer Protection, serving as 
Senior Deputy Sealer for 35 years. His work regularly required 
collaboration with federal agencies including the FBI, FDA, and 
Consumer Product Safety Commission to ensure the safety and fairness of 
products sold in our communities. He was recognized statewide with the 
``Great Guy Award'' by the Pennsylvania Association of Weights and 
Measures and presented annually to honor the top official in the 
Commonwealth.
  Mayor Cutchineal has lived a life defined by service, faith in 
community, and love for family. A lifelong resident of Tullytown and a 
1968 graduate of Pennsbury High School, he also served as a junior 
member of the Tullytown Fire Department. He later continued his 
education at Bucks County Community College studying Criminal Justice, 
followed by coursework at Temple University.
  Mayor Cutchineal shares his life's journey with his beloved wife, 
Cindy, to whom he has been married for 48 years. Together, they raised 
three children: Nick, Matt, and the late Kelly Elizabeth (Cutchineal) 
Hendryx, whose memory remains forever cherished. His devotion to his 
family has always remained at the center of his life, even as he 
dedicated thousands of hours to bettering the lives of others.
  As he closes this remarkable chapter of public service, we do not 
simply celebrate his retirement. We celebrate the example he has set. 
His dedication has strengthened not only a borough, but the very idea 
that local government can be a true force for good.
  Mr. Speaker, Mayor David Cutchineal's career stands as a testament to 
what hometown leadership should be. He is a leader who never asked for 
applause, a neighbor who always offered a hand, and a friend who 
believed that small towns deserve big-hearted champions. His legacy 
will continue to be felt in Tullytown for generations to come, 
especially from good governance and stronger public safety to 
patriotism embedded deep in the borough's civic spirit.
  On behalf of Pennsylvania's 1st District and the United States House 
of Representatives, I express my deepest appreciation to Mayor David 
Cutchineal for his service, his patriotism, and his enduring love of 
community. We wish him, Cindy, and the entire Cutchineal family 
happiness, peace, and every blessing in the years ahead.
