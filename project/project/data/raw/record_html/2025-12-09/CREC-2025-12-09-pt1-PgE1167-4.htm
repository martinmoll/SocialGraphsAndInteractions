               HONORING THE LATE LAWRENCE E. SAVAGE, SR.

                                  _____
                                 

                        HON. BENNIE G. THOMPSON

                             of mississippi

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, December 9, 2025

  MR. THOMPSON of Mississippi. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the 
life and legacy of Lawrence Edward Savage, Sr., an esteemed artist, 
community documentarian, husband, father, philosopher, and veteran, 
whose recent passing leaves a lasting mark on all who had the 
opportunity to experience his artistry and the inspiration he shared 
through his life.
  Mr. Savage was born in Memphis, Tennessee, on October 6, 1938, to 
Ruby Lundy (Savage) and Freddie Savage, and was raised in Chicago, 
Illinois. After completing high school, he served in the United States 
Army from 1958 to 1961, stationed at Ford Ord in California, and worked 
as a merchant seaman and barber. These experiences shaped his 
discipline and commitment to service.
  Upon returning home, Mr. Savage explored art as a hobby, eventually 
discovering his God-given talent. He began his professional

[[Page E1168]]

painting career at age 29 and, out of a desire to maintain 
authenticity, trained himself rather than follow conventional 
instruction. His early promise was recognized by several Art Institutes 
through merit scholarships.
  While living in Jackson, Mississippi, Mr. Savage thrived as an 
artist, producing over 500 portraits, more than 100 murals for 
churches, schools, and local businesses, and over 100 signs for small 
business owners. His work represented Mississippi in two state fairs 
and decorated the halls of the State Capitol, establishing him as a 
local artistic icon.
  He painted portraits of prominent figures such as Gladys Knight, 
James Brown, Senator Barack Obama, President Bill Clinton, and me, 
reflecting both his talent and his dedication to celebrating culture 
and leadership.
  In addition to his artistry, Mr. Savage was a self-taught musician 
who played guitar, piano, and harmonica, and a mentor who shared 
wisdom, guidance, and inspiration with everyone he encountered. He 
lived by a code of love, respect, and spiritual connection, seeing the 
divine spark in every person and nurturing his family and community 
through his example.
  Mr. Savage was a devoted husband to his wife of 53 years, JoAnn 
Smith-Savage, and a loving father and grandfather.
  Through his children, grandchildren, great-grandchildren, and his 
artwork, Lawrence Savage, Sr. leaves a profound legacy of creativity, 
vision, and love. His dedication to his craft, his family, and his 
community will continue to inspire countless Mississippians and 
Americans.
  Mr. Speaker, the passing of Lawrence Edward Savage, Sr. is a 
tremendous loss to his family, his community, and the many individuals 
and intitutions enriched by his art and mentorship. Yet his legacy 
endures--as a trailblazer in the arts, as a mentor and guide to 
countless individuals, and as a devoted family man who believed deeply 
in the power of creativity, love, and community.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring his memory, celebrating 
his remarkable contributions, and extending our heartfelt condolences 
to his family, friends, and all who knew and admired him.
