                  TRIBUTE TO MR. JAMES LEE ADAMS, JR.

                                  _____
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, October 3, 2025

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and remember a loving 
husband, dedicated father, trailblazing agricultural icon, dedicated 
community servant, dedicated man of God and dear friend of 
longstanding, Mr. James Lee Adams, Jr. Sadly, James Lee passed away on 
October 1, 2025. He leaves behind a legacy of service and commitment 
that will serve as an example for generations yet to come. His life 
will be remembered at a home-going service at the Camilla United 
Methodist Church in Camilla, Georgia on October 3, 2025, at 11:00 am.
  Born on January 2, 1943, in Camilla, Georgia to the union of James 
Lee Adams, Sr. and Ruth Borland Adams, James Lee was a proud 1965 
graduate of Georgia Tech where he earned a B.S. in Management. In 1966 
he earned his master's degree in Business Administration from Auburn 
University where he also taught undergraduate courses in Economics and 
Statistics. His professional career began at Lockheed Aircraft 
Corporation where he served in various capacities in administration.
  James returned to his roots in Camilla, where he joined his father in 
their farming operation. He embraced an array of advancements in 
agricultural technology, becoming a leader in precision agriculture and 
sustainable farming. His ingenuity set many records, including being 
the first in Georgia to achieve remarkable yields in corn and wheat. 
His influence reached far beyond our local community as he served in 
several national leadership roles, including as President of the 
American Soybean Association.
  Throughout his illustrious career, James Lee received numerous 
accolades, including being named one of the 100 most influential people 
in agriculture in the 20th century by Alpha Zeta and in 1992 the ``Man 
of the Year in Service to Southeastern Agriculture'' award from 
Progressive Farmer magazine. He was a true advocate for farmers, 
contributing significantly to policy and agricultural reforms at both 
the state and national levels. He assisted in writing several farm 
bills, including conservation, crop insurance and general commodity 
titles. He was twice selected as Farmer of the Year for Georgia, in 
1992 and 2000. In 2000, he was also selected as the Lancaster/Sunbelt 
Expo Southeastern Farmer of the Year--the first Georgia farmer to be 
honored with this award.
  It has been said that ``Service is the rent that we pay for the space 
that we occupy here on his earth.'' James Lee Adams paid his rent, and 
he paid it well. He was beloved not just for his accomplishments in 
agriculture but for his unwavering commitment to service. He was 
actively involved in local organizations, to include serving as 
President of the Camilla Rotary Club and twice leading the Chamber of 
Commerce. For more than 50 years, James Lee taught Sunday School and 
served as Chairman of the Deacons at First Baptist Church and Chairman 
of the Board at Camilla United Methodist Church, exemplifying his deep 
faith and service to God.
  His contributions to education, agriculture, and community service 
were immeasurable. He was a firm believer in the power of education and 
community engagement, taking an active role in founding Westwood 
Schools and serving on numerous other boards and committees.
  James Lee was united in marriage, first to Sue Martin, and their 
marriage lasted for 52 years. Together they had three beautiful 
daughters. James Lee found love again in his subsequent marriage to 
Barbara Ford Latham where he received Barbara's children into his life, 
accepting them as his own.
  On a personal note, I have known James Lee Adams since 1992 during my 
first run for Congress. He became my dear friend and supporter. In 
public life it is hard to find friends, and it is even harder to keep 
them. I can truly say that when I met James Lee Adams, I found a friend 
and I was blessed to keep him as my friend. He never told me what I 
wanted to hear but always told me what he felt I needed to hear. I will 
be forever grateful for the fidelity of his friendship and the timbre 
of his character.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues in the House of Representatives to 
join my wife, Vivian and me, along with the more than 765,000 people of 
Georgia's 2nd Congressional District in honoring the life and legacy of 
Mr. James Lee Adams, Jr. and in extending our deepest condolences to 
his family, friends, and all who mourn his loss. Moreover, we pray that 
they all will be consoled and comforted by an abiding faith and the 
Holy Spirit in the days, weeks and months ahead.
