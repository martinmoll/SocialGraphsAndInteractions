     HONORING FLYING TIGER MEL McMULLEN ON HIS 100TH BIRTHDAY AND 
           RECOGNIZING HIS EXTRAORDINARY SERVICE DURING WWII

                                 ______
                                 

                             HON. JUDY CHU

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                          Friday, June 6, 2025

  Ms CHU. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Mr. Mel McMullen of 
California on the occasion of his 100th birthday and in recognition of 
his extraordinary service during the Second World War.
  Born in Los Angeles on June 5, 1925, Mr. McMullen answered his 
country's call to service at the age of 17, Joining the U.S. Army Air 
Corps in 1943. By 1944, he was serving in the China-Burma-India Theater 
as a nose turret gunner and assistant engineer aboard a B-24J bomber 
named The Dragon Lady, as part of General Claire Chennault's famed 14th 
Air Force, the ``Flying Tigers.'' The legacy of the Flying Tigers is 
truly inspiring because it was made up of young men like Mr. McMullen 
who left their jobs to volunteer alongside the Chinese in defense of 
China, even before the U.S. officially entered the war against Japan. 
Despite not being an official part of the U.S. military, they were 
willing to risk their lives in the pursuit of peace. The Flying Tigers' 
achievements were truly extraordinary--operating with only a few dozen 
planes and a few hundred soldiers, they managed to bring down hundreds 
of Imperial Japanese aircraft, a testament to their bravery and skill.
  McMullen was assigned to the 425th Squadron of the 308th Bomb Group, 
flying long and dangerous missions across eastern China against 
Japanese forces. His squadron endured perilous flights over mountainous 
terrain, unescorted bombing runs under heavy anti-aircraft fire, and 
grueling conditions that tested their endurance and courage Mr. 
McMullen survived over 200 hours of aerial combat, a feat for which he 
was awarded both the Air Medal and the Distinguished Flying Cross.
  After the Flying Tigers were disbanded, Mr. McMullen continued 
serving in World War II by flying hazardous fuel transport missions 
over ``The Hump'' from India to China, a route so treacherous it was 
known as ``The Aluminum Trail'' for the wreckage of downed aircraft. 
This was a vital air transport lifeline for the Allies and Mr. 
McMullen's service reflects not only immense bravery but a profound 
commitment to his fellow airmen and to the mission of liberating Asia 
from imperial oppression.
  Beyond the battlefield, Mr. McMullen's life has remained a testament 
to leadership, service, and community. He served as National Commander 
of the China-Burma-India Veterans Association and as President of the 
Inland Empire Chapter of the Distinguished Flying Cross Society. He 
built a 43-year career in the title insurance industry and has been a 
devoted husband to his wife Jennifer for over 77 years. Together, they 
raised a family in Los Angeles and now enjoy the love of their 
grandchildren and a community they continue to serve.
  Mr. Speaker, Mel McMullen embodies the best of the American spirit: 
courage in the face of war, resilience in peacetime, and a life of 
meaning rooted in family and service. On behalf of a grateful Nation, I 
offer my congratulations on his 100th birthday and thank him for his 
tremendous sacrifices in defense of our freedoms. May his story 
continue to inspire future generations to serve with honor, humility, 
and purpose.
