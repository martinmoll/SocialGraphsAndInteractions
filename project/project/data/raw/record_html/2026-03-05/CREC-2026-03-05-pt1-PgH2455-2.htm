              WOMEN'S HISTORY MONTH: INTRODUCING ALYSA LIU

  (Ms. SIMON asked and was given permission to address the House for 1 
minute and to revise and extend her remarks.)
  Ms. SIMON. Mr. Speaker, this is Women's History Month, and I rise to 
introduce my constituent of the week. Her name is Alysa Liu. She is 20 
years old, and she was raised in Oakland, educated in Oakland, and 
trained in Oakland.
  Two weeks ago in Milan, Alysa brought home two Olympic gold medals to 
the United States of America. She is the first American woman on the 
top of that podium in 24 years. She walked away from skating at 16 and 
came back on her own terms.
  Her father fled China after Tiananmen Square, arrived in Oakland with 
nothing, worked his way through law school, and has raised five 
children on his own. He brought her to the ice at just 5 years old, and 
she took it from there.
  Watch her skate, and you see something extremely rare, Mr. Speaker, 
not just excellence but joy and clarity. She is a young woman so 
completely herself. An Olympic arena becomes a place of joy and 
freedom. That is an American hero, not despite where she came from but 
because of it.
  Oakland is proud, and we honor Alysa Liu today.
