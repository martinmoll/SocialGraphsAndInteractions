   HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF MRS. SARA CLEMENTINE JAMES BENSON

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Friday, December 12, 2025

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy of 
Mrs. Sara Clementine James Benson of Albany, Georgia, who passed from 
labor to reward on November 27, 2025. A devoted educator, community 
leader, and stalwart of faith, Mrs. Benson dedicated her life to the 
service of others and to the betterment of southwest Georgia. A service 
to celebrate her remarkable life will be held on Saturday, December 13, 
2025, at the Mount Zion Baptist Church in Albany, Georgia.
  Born on Easter Sunday, April 13, 1941, in Albany, Mrs. Benson--known 
to family and friends as Clem or Clemmie--was the daughter of the late 
Owen F. James, Sr., and Mary Harrell James. She graduated from Monroe 
High School in 1959 and, with the support of a full scholarship from 
the Las Amigas Civic and Social Club, earned her Bachelor of Science in 
Business Education from Albany State College in 1963. She went on to 
receive a Master of Business Education in 1969.
  For more than three decades Mrs. Benson shaped young minds and 
strengthened academic institutions. Mrs. Benson understood that 
``Education is the passport to the future, it belongs to those who 
prepare for it today.'' She served Albany State University with 
distinction--first as secretary for the Hazard Laboratory School and 
later as a valued member of the Business Education faculty. Upon her 
retirement in 1993, she was honored with the title Assistant Professor 
Emeritus of the School of Business. She continued to share her 
knowledge as an adjunct instructor at Albany Technical College.
  A committed sorority sister and civic volunteer. Mrs. Benson was 
initiated into Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority, Incorporated, Gamma Sigma 
Chapter in 1962 and later served in Delta Eta Omega Chapter in numerous 
leadership roles. She was a Golden Soror with more than fifty years of 
service. Someone once said that, ``Service is the rent that we pay for 
the space that we occupy here on this earth.'' Mrs. Benson paid her 
rent and she paid it well. Her civic involvement extended to Las Amigas 
Civic and Social Club, the Albany State University National Alumni 
Association, the Southwest Georgia Association for Convalescent and 
Aging Persons, and many other local organizations. She was very giving 
and never shy about asking others to give to the causes that she cared 
deeply about. She was very persuasive and would not take ``no'' for an 
answer.
  Mrs. Benson's faith and service were deeply rooted in Mt. Zion 
Baptist Church, where she

[[Page E1191]]

provided leadership in New Members Orientation, the General Missionary 
Ministry, the Floral Club, Benevolence Ministry, and the Prison 
Ministry. A lifelong learner and warm companion, she enjoyed reading, 
bowling, playing the piano, and card games--always guided by the 
philosophy to ``lift as you climb.'' The great agricultural chemist 
George Washington Carver once said that ``How far you go in life 
depends on your being tender with young, compassionate with the aged, 
sympathetic with the striving and tolerant of the weak and the strong, 
because someday in life you will have been all of these.'' Mrs. Benson 
went far in life because she treated all people with dignity and 
respect and always reached her hand back to help others.
  Mrs. Benson leaves behind her husband of 59 years, Winfred Benson, 
Sr.; her daughter, Thedjuana (Jerry) Nicholson; a sister, Juanita 
James; three granddaughters, four great-granddaughters, a great-great-
grandson; and a wide circle of family, friends, colleagues, and 
neighbors who will cherish her memory.
  Mr. Speaker, Sara Benson's devoted work as an educator, mentor, and 
community servant made a lasting difference in Albany and beyond. I ask 
my colleagues to join my wife Vivian and me, along with the more than 
765,000 people of Georgia's 2nd Congressional District in honoring the 
life and legacy of Sara Clementine James Benson.
  I join her family and the people of southwest Georgia in mourning her 
passing and in celebrating a life lived in service to others. May her 
example continue to inspire our communities to educate, uplift, and 
care for one another. We pray that all who knew and loved her will be 
consoled and comforted by an abiding faith and the Holy Spirit in the 
weeks, months, and years to come.
