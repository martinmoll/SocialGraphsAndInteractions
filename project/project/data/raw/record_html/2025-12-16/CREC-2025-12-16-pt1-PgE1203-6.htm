                RECOGNIZING MR. MITCHELL GEORGE ANGELOS

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. SCOTT PERRY

                            of pennsylvania

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, December 16, 2025

  Mr. PERRY. Mr. Speaker, I am honored and humbled to recognize Mr. 
Mitchell George Angelos of York, Pennsylvania, upon the auspicious 
occasion of his 100th Birthday.
  Mr. Angelos' life has been dedicated to the service of our Nation. 
Knowing that he likely would be drafted for World War II, he finished 
high school in three years. He was, indeed, drafted in August 1944, and 
reported to Engineering Basic Training at Fort Belvoir, Virginia, where 
he was taught how to be an Infantryman and build Bailey modular 
bridges. Next,

[[Page E1204]]

he went to Demolition Specialist School and learned how to lay mines, 
booby traps, and defuse those of the enemies.
  At the end of his training, Mr. Angelos developed a severe infection 
and was taken to an Army hospital; thus separating him from his unit, 
which deployed to Europe while he recovered. After landing in La Havre, 
France, in 1945, he learned that many of his comrades from boot camp 
had perished in the Battle of the Bulge.
  On a train to Belgium, the Soldiers were tightly packed in boxcars; 
as Mr. Angelos put it, like a ``herd of cattle.'' After finally 
arriving in Verviers, Belgium, he fell ill and walked about a mile to 
sick bay; ultimately being hospitalized while the rest of the Soldiers 
in his unit moved on.
  Eventually, Mr. Angelos landed in Marseilles, France, where his 
battalion prepared to deploy to the Pacific, and where he learned that 
the first atom bomb was dropped. Post-war, he was assigned to the Labor 
Supervision Battalion at Camp Calle, France, where he supervised 200 
German Prisoners of War (POW) for the next seven months. He learned how 
to drive there, and his instructor was a German POW who ``borrowed'' a 
Jeep from the Camp motor pool. Another German POW made him some trench 
art--a small, model boat about 24 inches long--that he has to this day.
  After the War, Mr. Angelos earned a degree in Mechanical Engineering 
from the University of Pittsburgh, graduating at the top of his class. 
During his career, he worked primarily in the military field, was 
considered an expert on fuses, and holds 7 U.S. Patents in his name. He 
was the Program Manager for the M-456 HEAT 105mm artillery round--the 
precursor to the HEAT round used in today's M1 Abrams tank.
  Mr. Speaker, I am humbled and privileged to recognize Mr. Mitchell 
George Angelos on the great occasion of his 100th Birthday, as well as 
his tireless and selfless service to our Nation during World War II; 
truly he is the epitome of The Greatest Generation. His is an example 
of a ``life done right''--as evidenced by a family and many friends who 
love him dearly, and the countless people whose lives he has affected 
in myriad ways. He sets the standard by which we all should be judged.
