# Text take-aways for the pruning pass (from Sekara et al., PNAS 2016)

Written 25 Nov 2026. Apply these together with the group's printed feedback on the full draft
(`two_maps_full_draft_overleaf/main.tex`). They are about how much space each section gets, not about content.
Reference paper: Sekara, Stopczynski & Lehmann (2016), *Fundamental structures of dynamic social networks*,
PNAS 113(36). Same template as ours, 6 pages, 4 figures.

| Section | Sekara et al. | Our draft (25 Nov) |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | ~230 words | ~165 (course: ~150) |
| Significance | ~120 words | ~115 |
| Introduction | ~2/3 column, 5 short paragraphs | ~1.2 columns; last paragraph previews every result |
| Results | ~3.5 pages; run-in headings of 1-3 words | ~3 pages; headings are full claims, often 2 lines |
| Discussion | ~1 column | ~1.5 columns |
| Methods | ~1/3 column (data, consent, one definition); the rest is "SI Appendix, S3..." | ~1.5 pages, 13 subsections |
| Author contributions | 1 line, PNAS form | 9 lines |
| References | 29 | 21 |

1. **Methods is the biggest lever.** He reports what was done and moves how it was checked to the SI. Most of
   our Methods is checking (matcher precision, yield rule, coder agreement, bill links). Open question: does the
   course allow an SI or appendix outside the 6 pages, or only a pointer to the repo? Check the course page first.
2. **Author contributions.** The course requires "specific, per person", so we keep more than he has, but in the
   compact PNAS form ("M.M. built ...; A.M. collected ...; D.A.M. coded ...").
3. **Short subheadings.** Run-in labels of 1-3 words instead of sentence-long claims: one line saved per heading.
4. **One headline number per paragraph.** Put robustness in one sentence or the Methods. The Fig 3 caption
   repeats the list of checks that is already in the Results text.
5. **Captions say what is shown.** 4-8 lines: one clause per panel and one closing take-away; no Methods
   detail.
